One of the four 1988 bishops; expelled from the SSPX in 2012 after public defiance; led the 'Resistance', for which he consecrated six bishops without mandate (2015–2022); his 2009 Holocaust denial caused a worldwide scandal.[4] [7] [12]

Recorded events

11 dated events in this chronology name this figure or have them as their unambiguous actor. A ? flag marks a date not yet verified against a primary source.

YearDatePlaceEvent
1988 1988-06-30 Écône The Écône consecrations — Lefebvre, with Bishop Antônio de Castro Mayer (emeritus of Campos, Brazil) as co-consecrator, consecrates Bernard Fellay, Bernard Tissier de Mallerais, Richard Williamson and Alfonso de Galarreta without papal mandate.[1]
1988 1988-07-01 Rome Excommunications declared — The Congregation for Bishops declares that Lefebvre, Castro Mayer and the four new bishops incurred latae sententiae excommunication.[1] [5]
1991 1991-07-28 São Fidélis, Brazil Rangel consecrated for the Campos union — Licínio Rangel is consecrated without papal mandate by Bishop Tissier de Mallerais, assisted by de Galarreta and Williamson, to lead the Campos priests after Castro Mayer's death.[8] [9]
2009 2009-01 Worldwide Williamson affair — A television interview in which Bishop Richard Williamson denied the Holocaust airs just before the remission is announced, causing worldwide controversy; the SSPX removes him as rector of the La Reja seminary.[4]
2009 2009-01-21 Rome 1988 excommunications remitted — A decree of the Congregation for Bishops (dated 21 January, published 24 January) lifts the excommunications of the four bishops; some sources cite 10 March 2009, the date of Benedict XVI's explanatory letter.[6]
2012 2012-10-04 Menzingen Williamson expelled — The SSPX expels Bishop Williamson after unauthorized confirmations in Brazil and public defiance; he goes on to lead the loose 'Resistance' network.[4]
2015 2015-03-19 Nova Friburgo, Brazil Resistance consecration in Brazil — Williamson consecrates Jean-Michel Faure without mandate at the Santa Cruz Monastery, consolidating the 'Resistance' splinter. The local ordinary of Nova Friburgo, Bishop Edney Gouvêa Mattoso, and canonical commentators state that a new latae sententiae excommunication (can. 1382) was thereby incurred; no Holy See statement on the individuals has been located.[4] [13]
2016 2016-03-19 Nova Friburgo, Brazil Second Resistance consecration in Brazil — Williamson consecrates Tomás de Aquino Ferreira da Costa, prior of the Santa Cruz Monastery, without mandate.[11]
2017 2017-05-11 Diocese of Arlington, Virginia, USA Zendejas consecrated for the Resistance — Williamson, assisted by Faure and Tomás de Aquino, consecrates Gerardo Zendejas; the local bishop's statement notes the excommunication incurred.[10]
2022 2022 Cork, Ireland / Poland Later Resistance consecrations — Williamson consecrates Paul Morgan in secret at Cork (14 February 2022), with Giacomo Ballini — whose own private 2021 consecration Williamson announced only in December 2022 — as co-consecrator, and Michał Stobnicki in Poland (15 August 2022), bringing to six the bishops he consecrated for the 'Resistance' between 2015 and 2022.[4]
2025 2025-01-29 Margate, England Death of Richard Williamson — The expelled 1988 bishop and 'Resistance' leader dies at 84.[7]
