YearDatePlaceEvent
1988 1988-06-30 Écône The Écône consecrations — Lefebvre, with Bishop Antônio de Castro Mayer (emeritus of Campos, Brazil) as co-consecrator, consecrates Bernard Fellay, Bernard Tissier de Mallerais, Richard Williamson and Alfonso de Galarreta without papal mandate.[1]
1988 1988-07-01 Rome Excommunications declared — The Congregation for Bishops declares that Lefebvre, Castro Mayer and the four new bishops incurred latae sententiae excommunication.[1] [4]
1991 1991-07-28 São Fidélis, Brazil Rangel consecrated for the Campos union — Licínio Rangel is consecrated without papal mandate by Bishop Tissier de Mallerais, assisted by de Galarreta and Williamson, to lead the Campos priests after Castro Mayer's death.[10] [11]
2009 2009-01-21 Rome 1988 excommunications remitted — A decree of the Congregation for Bishops (dated 21 January, published 24 January) lifts the excommunications of the four bishops; some sources cite 10 March 2009, the date of Benedict XVI's explanatory letter.[5]
2026 2026-07-01 Écône The 2026 Écône consecrations — Bishops de Galarreta and Fellay consecrate four new bishops — Pascal Schreiber, Michael Goldade, Michel Poinsinet de Sivry and Marc Hanappier — without papal mandate. The Society's own figure, relayed by Wikipedia, is some 15,000 faithful together with about 1,300 priests and religious; press estimates circulated at higher numbers and are not reconciled here.[3] [6]
2026 2026-07-02 Rome New excommunications declared — A DDF decree declares the six bishops involved excommunicated latae sententiae, describes SSPX clergy as being in schism, and revokes the 2015–2017 confession and marriage provisions; the SSPX rejects the decree as 'objectively unjust and invalid'. Press summaries differ on how the decree treats priests and laity — each reading is attributed.[7] [8] [9]
