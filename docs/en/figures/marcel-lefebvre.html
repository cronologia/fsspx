Dossier

This page gathers what this chronology records about one figure — the role summary, the events linked to them, and their position in the episcopal genealogy — with every claim carrying its source. It is a dossier page, not a full biography.

Founder and first Superior General (1970–1982); former Archbishop of Dakar and Superior General of the Holy Ghost Fathers; suspended a divinis 1976; consecrated four bishops in 1988 and was declared excommunicated; buried at Écône.[2]

Recorded events

15 dated events in this chronology name this figure or have them as their unambiguous actor. A ? flag marks a date not yet verified against a primary source.

YearDatePlaceEvent
1970 1970-11-01 Fribourg, Switzerland Founding of the SSPX — Bishop François Charrière erects the Society as a pia unio on an ad experimentum basis for six years; Marcel Lefebvre is founder and superior.[1]
1974 1974-11-21 Écône Lefebvre's Declaration — Lefebvre professes adherence to 'eternal Rome' while rejecting 'the Rome of neo-modernist tendencies' — the text that became the Society's ideological charter. The date is verified against the official text published by the SSPX General House, dated 21 November 1974.[7] [1]
1976 1976-06-29 Écône The 'hot summer' ordinations — Lefebvre ordains priests despite an explicit prohibition, after being publicly rebuked by Paul VI in consistory on 24 May.[1]
1976 1976-07-22 Rome Suspension a divinis — After a 1 July suspension from conferring orders and continued refusal to comply, Lefebvre is suspended from all sacramental ministry.[1]
1976 1976-08-29 Lille, France Lefebvre's Lille sermon — Weeks after his suspension a divinis, Lefebvre preaches in a hangar at the Lille sports palace to a crowd La Porte Latine puts at about 7,000. Of the reformed rites he says: “Le rite de la nouvelle messe est un rite bâtard. Les sacrements sont des sacrements bâtards” — ‘the rite of the new Mass is a bastard rite; the sacraments are bastard sacraments’ — and “Les prêtres qui sortent des séminaires sont des prêtres bâtards.” The epithet falls on the rite, the sacraments and the priests; the sermon does not call the Mass itself ‘bastard’, a distinction later paraphrases have not always kept.[11]
1976 1976-09-11 Castel Gandolfo Paul VI receives Lefebvre at Castel Gandolfo — Paul VI receives Lefebvre privately at the papal villa. According to the record kept by the assistant secretary of state who attended, published in 2018 by Mons. Leonardo Sapienza in ‘La Barca di Paolo’ and reported by Catholic News Service, the Pope told him: “Unfortunately, the position you have taken is that of an antipope.” The line is often relayed as having been sent in a letter; on this account it was spoken at this meeting.[12]
1977 1977 Écône Guérard des Lauriers dismissed from Écône — Fr. Michel-Louis Guérard des Lauriers OP, professor of theology at the seminary from 1971, is dismissed in autumn 1977 after teaching the vacant-see thesis to seminarians in a September retreat despite Lefebvre's prohibition — the SSPX account (Si Si No No, 1998) and his sedeprivationist biographer (IMBC) agree on the dismissal and its ground. Consecrated by Thục in 1981, he became the source of the sedeprivationist ('Cassiciacum') lineages — see the episcopal genealogy.[9] [10]
1978 1978-11 ? Rome Lefebvre meets John Paul II — Shortly after the papal election, Lefebvre seeks regularization; talks continue intermittently through the 1980s.[2]
1982 1982 Switzerland Schmidberger succeeds Lefebvre — Fr. Franz Schmidberger becomes Superior General (1982–1994); Lefebvre remains the Society's episcopal head.[1]
1987 1987-06 Écône Consecrations announced — Lefebvre announces his intention to consecrate bishops without a papal mandate, citing his age and the need to continue ordinations.[1]
1988 1988-05-05 Rome Protocol signed — and withdrawn — Lefebvre and Cardinal Ratzinger sign a protocol of agreement on 5 May. On 6 May Lefebvre writes to Ratzinger announcing he will proceed with episcopal consecrations by 30 June (letter text published by SSPX Asia); 'Ecclesia Dei' treats his letter of 2 June 1988 as the definitive refusal.[1] [8] [5]
1988 1988-06-30 Écône The Écône consecrations — Lefebvre, with Bishop Antônio de Castro Mayer (emeritus of Campos, Brazil) as co-consecrator, consecrates Bernard Fellay, Bernard Tissier de Mallerais, Richard Williamson and Alfonso de Galarreta without papal mandate.[1]
1988 1988-07-01 Rome Excommunications declared — The Congregation for Bishops declares that Lefebvre, Castro Mayer and the four new bishops incurred latae sententiae excommunication.[1] [5]
1991 1991-03-25 Martigny, Switzerland Death of Marcel Lefebvre — The founder dies at 85 and is buried at the Écône seminary.[2]
1996 1996-08-24 Rome Explanatory note on adherence to the Lefebvre movement — The Pontifical Council for the Interpretation of Legislative Texts issues an explanatory note on the excommunication for schism incurred by those who adhere to Archbishop Lefebvre's movement. It requires for formal adherence both an internal element — “a free and informed agreement with the substance of the schism… which puts such an option above obedience to the Pope” — and an external one, “the most manifest sign of which will be the exclusive participation in Lefebvrian ‘ecclesial’ acts, without taking part in the acts of the Catholic Church”; it adds that “an occasional participation in liturgical acts or the activity of the Lefebvrian movement, done without making one's own the attitude of doctrinal and disciplinary disunion of such a movement, does not suffice”, such cases being judged individually. The Dicastery for the Doctrine of the Faith cites the note as still in force in 2026.[13] [14]
