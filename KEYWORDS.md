Notes a searcher needs:

- **`Dom Marcel` is the Brazilian form.** Brazilian sources — both the COF
  lectures and the Portuguese transcripts — say `Dom Marcel`, frequently with
  the surname omitted or destroyed. If you search only for surnames you will
  miss the Brazilian material entirely, which is exactly the audience this
  project is built for.
- **`Monsenhor` (PT) / `Mgr` (FR) / `Archbishop` (EN) / `Monsignor`** are four
  different honorifics for the same man. `Archbishop Lefebvre` returns 1 hit in
  121 transcripts; `Monsenhor Lefeb*` returns 4; neither is the main form.
- **Latin.** `Fraternitas Sacerdotalis Sancti Pii X` is the form on Latin
  canonical documents; it is attested in this repo's [`context.md`](context.md)
  and returns **0** in the vault. Search it against `vatican.va`, not the vault.
- Derived adjectives are their own search terms and are often the *only* thing
  present: `lefebvrista` / `lefebvriano` / `lefebvrismo` and their many ASR
  spellings (§3), plus the Spanish polemical label `Lefebvritólicos`
  (transcript 97; ticket #32 records it as *reception data — attributed, never
  adopted*).

### 4. Ambiguous referents and false friends

Same string, different subject. Read the sentence before counting the hit.

- **`Lefebvre` → Henri Lefebvre.** COF226 names *Henri* Lefebvre, the French
  Marxist sociologist, alongside Lucien Goldmann. Nothing to do with the
  archbishop. One of 7 COF files carrying the exact string is the wrong man.
- **`Pio X` → Pope St Pius X.** 23 COF files contain `Pio X`; only 6 touch the
  Society. COF069's `a missa de São Pio X, São Pio V` is about **Mass rites and
  the popes they are named for**, not the Society — the one file that a
  `São Pio X` search adds beyond the real five is a false positive. In the
  transcripts, `Pio X` (156) also collides with `Pio XI` and `Pio XII`, and the
  ASR emits `Pio Xo` (16), `Pio Xº`, `Pio Ximo`, `Pio Xa`.
- **`Bento X` → Benedict XVI, not Benedict X.** The ASR truncation collides
  with the name of an 11th-century antipope. In this corpus it is always
  Ratzinger; in a wider search it will not be.
- **`tarifa` → *tariqa*, or a tariff.** `tarifa` is an ordinary Portuguese word
  (fare/tariff) *and* the commonest ASR spelling of `tariqa`. Transcript 58's
  "*Fui expulso da tarifa em 1987*" is the Sufi order; transcript 46's
  "*interrompido nem nas tarifas*" is not. There is no way to tell without the
  sentence.
- **`econ*` → economia.** A loose search for `Écône` in Portuguese drowns:
  `econ` (76), `economia` (55), `econom` (16), `ecol*`, `ecos`. `Econe` — the
  needle — has 4 hits.
- **`Campos` → three different things.** The Campos (RJ) traditionalist diocese
  and its Apostolic Administration (this repo's subject); **São José dos
  Campos**, an unrelated city (transcript 113, a Foro de São Paulo interview);
  and the common noun *campos* (fields). 41 of the ~68 transcript hits sit in
  transcript 1 alone, which *is* about the Society.
- **`Dom Marcelo` → sometimes Lefebvre, sometimes not.** `Dom Marcelo Lefebre`
  (COF301) and `Dom Marcelo Lefreve` (t6) are the archbishop; transcript 1's
  "*Dom Marcelo fala que é a terceira guerra*" and transcript 121's "*bravo com
  o Dom Marcelo*" are somebody else.
- **`resistência` → the Williamson network, or the ordinary noun.** This repo's
  `organizations[4]` is the "Resistance". Transcript 7's "*flertou com a
  resistência*" is that network; transcript 1's "*resistência às reformas*" is
  plain Portuguese.
- **`tradicionalista` (186 hits, 35 files) covers three unrelated movements.**
  Catholic traditionalism, the Guénon–Schuon Traditionalist School, and Evola's
  political Traditionalism. See `[[traditionalist-school]]` and §6.
- **A fuzzy `lef*`/`laf*` regex over the Spanish transcripts pulls `la Far`
  (7) and `lafar` (3)** — the FARC, not Lefebvre. Widen the pattern and you buy
  this noise.

### 5. Terms of art — do not translate when searching

The Latin is the canonical string in Vatican documents and in this dataset's
`[[term-id]]` markers. Portuguese and Spanish sources translate it, garble it,
or skip it — so the Latin finds the primary sources and the vernacular finds the
commentary. **Search both; never substitute one for the other.** Definitions
live in `cronologia/glossary`, not here.
